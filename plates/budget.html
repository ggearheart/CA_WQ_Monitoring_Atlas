Budget: flat for twenty years, then cut by 62 percent

SWAMP buys most of its monitoring through contracts with laboratories, universities and field crews. That contract budget was $5.8 million in 2004 and $5.8 million in 2024. Prices rose 66 percent in between. Then the budget fell to $2.2 million.

Less money, fewer samples

The Water Boards' public performance report counts every SWAMP site visit. Visits fell by more than half between 2012-13 and 2024-25, before most of the 2024 reduction had taken effect.

What happened in 2024

In August 2024 two statewide budget letters told every department to eliminate vacant positions and to cut operating budgets by 7.95 percent. At the Water Boards much of that reduction landed on contract funds, which is where monitoring lives. SWAMP lost one staff position and 62 percent of its contract budget.

Eliminated statewide

  • Bioaccumulation Monitoring Program. The fish-tissue testing behind California's "safe to eat" advisories.
  • All Regional Water Board monitoring allocations (November 2024), followed by regional discretionary contract funds (June 2025).

Still funded, at reduced reach

  • Bioassessment
  • Stream Pollution Trends
  • Freshwater harmful algal bloom response
  • Data system and quality assurance

What stopped, region by region

Regional Water BoardWhat was lost
1 · North Coast$320,000 in SWAMP funds and $325,000 in discretionary funds over two years. All Elk River projects but one cancelled. No reserve for spill and wildfire sampling. Mercury-in-fish sampling at Lakes Mendocino and Sonoma dropped. Bacteria studies on the Russian and Shasta Rivers and Stemple Creek shelved.
2 · San Francisco BayField team went from five people to one. More than 60 percent of lab funds gone ($159,000 a year). Ended 14 years in the Watershed Stewards intern program. Sport-fish monitoring for Bay health advisories stopped.
3 · Central CoastAbout $530,000, roughly half the regional program. Fewer sites, fewer visits, fewer pollutants tested. Long-term trend analysis interrupted.
4 · Los AngelesNo funds for studies on subsistence fishing and tribal uses, regional stream bioassessment, or any statewide SWAMP survey.
5 · Central ValleyEnded a 20-year monitoring partnership with the Department of Water Resources that had been tracking wildfire effects.
6 · Lahontan2026 fish-tissue monitoring cancelled. Stream bioassessment contracts removed. A long-running quarterly trend series for low-level contaminants ended.
7 · Colorado RiverNo routine SWAMP monitoring since spring 2025. Algal bloom checks only when a bloom is reported or before holidays.
9 · San DiegoNo retesting of fish and shellfish for PFAS. No monitoring to support tribal and subsistence fishing uses. No capacity for unplanned sampling after spills or fish kills.

Takeaway

The 2024 reduction was not a trim to a healthy program. It came after twenty years in which inflation had already removed 40 percent of the program's capacity, and it removed most of what was left.
