           SUBJECT  :    WATER QUALITY: INFORMATION
            SUMMARY  :    
            Existing law  establishes a system of water quality control,  
           which includes the compilation and dissemination of  
           information on the beneficial uses of the state's waters,  
           water quality objectives, and other information about water  
           quality pursuant to the Porter-Cologne Water Quality Control  
           Act.
            This bill  :
           1) Requires the state board to incorporate within its public  
              education program additional information on standards,  
              regulations, and enforcement and to have these posted on  
              its Web site in a publicly accessible manner.
              a)    The information shall encompass information on  
                 permits, waste discharge requirements, waivers, reports,  
                 lists, chars, standards, objectives, agreements,  
                 enforcement actions, and other types of referenced  
                 information.
              b)    The state board shall ensure that information is  
                 available in single locations, to the extent available  
                 resources make this practicable.
           2) Repeals an obsolete provision that required an inventory of  
              water quality monitoring activities for coastal waters by  
              January 1, 2000 and replaces it with a new provision  
              requiring CalEPA and the Resources Agency to enter into a  
              memorandum of understanding (MOU) to establish the  
                                                               SB 1070
                                                                 Page 2
              California Water Quality Monitoring Council, to be  
              administered by the state board.  The MOU shall include the  
              following:
              a)    Reduce redundancies, inefficiencies, and inadequacies  
                 of data collection programs;
              b)    Ensure the collection of sufficient information to  
                 track improvements in water quality and evaluate the  
                 overall effectiveness of state board and regional board  
                 programs and specified water quality projects;
           3) Requires CalEPA and the Resources Agency to jointly  
              determine the composition of the Monitoring Council; and  
              for the Council to conduct the following activities:
              a)    To coordinate water quality monitoring efforts with  
                 specified agencies, groups and parties to develop a  
                 coordinated, integrated, and comprehensive network for  
                 collecting disseminating water quality information.
              b)    To provide annual reports to CalEPA and the Resources  
                 Agency on recommendations for maximizing the efficiency  
                 and effectiveness of water quality data collection and  
                 dissemination.
              c)    To consult with US EPA in preparing recommendations  
                 and to make recommendations and responses by CalEPA or  
                 the Resources Agency publicly available via the  
                 Internet.
              d)    To complete an inventory of existing water quality  
                 monitoring and data collections efforts and making  
                 information publicly available by March 1, 2007.
           4) Requires all state entities, including institutions of  
              higher education collecting water quality information to  
              cooperate with the state board in the development and  
              implementation of the MOU to develop the water quality  
              network.
           5) Requires the network of the monitoring council  to include  
              the following components:
                                                               SB 1070
                                                                 Page 3
              a)    The development and implementation of a strategy  
                 within 10 years from the date of adoption with specified  
                 elements.
              b)    The implementation of water quality protection  
                 indicators with specified elements.
              c)    Additional components include quality management  
                 plans, methodological elements, data system elements,  
                 water quality reports,  surface water ambient monitoring  
                 program needs assessment, and public access to  
                 information.
           6) Requires the Secretary of CalEPA to conduct a biennial  
              audit of the effectiveness of monitoring and assessment  
              network and the monitoring council by December 1, 2008,  
              including a specified assessment of program effectiveness.
           7) Requires the Secretary of Cal EPA to consult with the  
              Secretary of the Resources Agency in preparing the audit.
           8) Requires the state board to prioritize the use of federal  
              funds for specified monitoring activities.
           9) Prohibits the state board from using more than 5 percent of  
              funds for administrative costs connected to this section.
            COMMENTS  :
            1) Purpose of Bill  .  According to the author's office, "in its  
              most recent monitoring report to the US EPA, California  
              only reports on the health of 22% of its coastal shoreline,  
              34% of its lakes and reservoirs, and a mere 15% of its  
              rivers and streams?..SB 1070 would provide a complete,  
              coordinated picture of current and changing water quality  
              throughout the state, inform the public with regard to safe  
              use of its waters, and report back to the public on the  
              success of efforts to protect and restore those waters?..    
              Better information is needed to produce better  
              decision-making, and to allow the public to make  
              well-informed choices about using and managing its waters.  
              ?. By coordinating monitoring efforts, the public and  
                                                               SB 1070
                                                                 Page 4
              decision-makers will better use scarce funds, obtain better  
              information from existing activities, and be able to  
              identify gaps that can be filled with additional, carefully  
              targeted monitoring projects."
            2) Background  .  Funds for water quality monitoring have  
              dropped significantly since the 1980s and are currently  
              inadequate to provide an overall assessment of the health  
              of all of the state's waterways.  For example, the state's  
              most recent biennial water quality report to the US EPA  
              indicated that the health of fully 85% of the state's  
              rivers and streams is not monitored.  Because of a lack of  
              resources, monitoring has been focused on those areas of  
              high priority, such as impaired waterways.  As a result,  
              many waters that may be degrading or otherwise in need of  
              protection are being ignored.  In addition, the lack of a  
              comprehensive, coordinated statewide monitoring effort  
              means that the impacts of new programs to control pollution  
              - such as projects funded by Propositions 40 and 50 as well  
              as new agricultural and silvicultural runoff initiatives,  
              are going essentially unmeasured.
