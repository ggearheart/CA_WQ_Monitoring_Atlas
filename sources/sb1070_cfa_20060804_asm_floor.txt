           SUMMARY  :  Requires the establishment of the California Water  
          Quality Monitoring Council (Council).  Specifically,  this bill  :
          1)Repeals an obsolete provision of law and replaces it with a  
            new provision requiring the California Environmental  
            Protection Agency (CalEPA) and the Resources Agency to enter  
            into a memorandum of understanding (MOU) by December 1, 2007,  
            to establish the Council to be administered by the State Water  
            Resources Control Board (SWRCB).
          2)Requires SWRCB, in coordination with the Council, to develop a  
            comprehensive monitoring program, using and expanding upon the  
            states existing statewide regional and other monitoring  
            capabilities.  
          3)Requires SWRCB, with the assistance of the regional water  
            quality control boards (RWQCBs), to incorporate within its  
            public education program additional information on standards,  
            regulations, and enforcement and to have these posted on its  
            internet site in a publicly accessible manner.
          4)Requires SWRCB to administer the Council, and requires CalEPA  
            to submit a triennial audit on the effectiveness of a  
                                                                  SB 1070
                                                                  Page  2
            specified monitoring and assessment network developed and  
            implemented by the Council.
          5)Requires SWRCB to identify the full costs of implementation of  
            the comprehensive monitoring program strategy and identify  
            sources of funding.
          6)Identifies one specific source of fee funding, Water Code  
            Section 13260(d)(1), that may be used for implementation of  
            the program to the extent that the fee is in the amount  
            necessary to recover the costs incurred by SWRCB and RWQCBs in  
            connection with waste discharge requirements and waivers of  
            waste discharge requirements.  
           EXISTING LAW  , pursuant to the Porter-Cologne Water Quality  
          Control Act (Act), establishes a system of water quality  
          control, which includes the compilation and dissemination of  
          information on the beneficial uses of the state's waters, water  
          quality objectives, and other information about water quality.
          FISCAL EFFECT  :  According to the Assembly Appropriations  
          Committee analysis, moderate costs, perhaps $250,000 every two  
          years starting in fiscal year (FY) 2008-09, to CalEPA to conduct  
          the water quality monitoring audit; minor ongoing costs, likely  
          less than $25,000 starting in FY 2006-07, to SWRCB to gather and  
          place water quality-related information on its Web site.   
          (General Fund or Waste Discharge Permit Fund.)  However, the  
          June 2006 amendments to this bill should have the effect of  
          reducing these costs because they reduce the frequency of the  
          water quality monitoring audit by making it a triennial rather  
          biennial audit.
           COMMENTS  :
          1)According to the author's office, "in its most recent  
            monitoring report to the United States  Environmental  
            Protection Agency, California only reports on the health of  
            22% of its coastal shoreline, 34% of its lakes and reservoirs,  
            and a mere 15% of its rivers and streams.  Put another way,  
            the health of 85% of the state's rivers and streams is not  
            being monitored."
          2)Leveraging and coordinating existing programs and resources:   
            This bill requires CalEPA and the Resources Agency to enter  
            into a memorandum of understanding by December 1, 2006, for  
                                                                  SB 1070
                                                                  Page  3
            the purposes of establishing the Council.  It requires SWRCB  
            to administer the Council, and requires CalEPA to submit a  
            biennial audit on the effectiveness of a specified monitoring  
            and assessment network developed and implemented by the  
            Council.  CalEPA estimates auditing costs to be about  
            $222,000.
          3)This Council includes local, state and federal agencies,  
            private industry, and citizen groups that collect monitoring  
            data.  The Council will coordinate these efforts to provide a  
            more complete picture of water health throughout the state,  
            and will report back to the public on the success of  
            investments to restore and protect those waters.  This effort  
            will increase efficiency by leveraging existing monitoring  
            activities and ensuring the data collected can be used  
            broadly.  This bill will also provide accountability to the  
            public about the health of its waters by requiring existing  
            electronic information, including regulatory information  
            already available to the government, to be provided to the  
            public via the Internet, so that the public can be a more  
            effective partner in protecting the health of their waters.
          4)Role of SWRCB:  As the administrator of the Council, SWRCB  
            would be required to design the network described in this  
            bill, coordinate activities, conduct a statewide inventory,  
            write annual reports, post information on the Internet, and  
            develop a strategy and network that is to be phased in over 10  
            years.
          5)The July 2006 floor amendments taken in the Assembly are  
            primarily technical and clarifying in nature.  The amended  
            bill is consistent with the policy as passed out of the  
            Assembly Environmental Safety and Toxic Materials Committee:   
            it promotes the coordination and leveraging of existing state  
            programs to achieve better protection of the state's water  
            quality.
